Methodology: how we chose the sales priorities

What we reviewed. 135 source-linked public records, grouped into 44 use-case families, captured on 31 October 2026.

How we chose. We prioritised concrete deployment evidence, stronger source attribution, recurring operating problems and a clear buyer with a measurable outcome. Customer-owned accounts and named customer discussions carry more weight than anonymous cases, announcements or demonstrations. The cloud shows record volume; publication counts alone do not determine the sales priorities, and records can overlap.

Industrial reliability: high confidence as a starting theme. Holcim reports predictive maintenance deployed at 45 plants, while Dow describes starting with reliability and extending its use. These concrete examples support cement and chemicals as initial targets, with reliability leaders, plant managers and operations directors as the proposed buyers.

Food demand planning: moderate confidence as the next focus. A detailed food-processing case describes the planning workflow, data integration and reported forecast improvements. Its anonymous, vendor-reported evidence gives less assurance. Demand-planning and supply-chain leaders are the proposed buyers.

What confidence means. These are well-supported places to start sales conversations. Current account pain, buyer access, data readiness and buying timing determine where sustained sales pressure belongs. Published outcomes are not a forecast of another company's results.

Word size reflects catalogue-record counts, not market size or buying intent. Publications and programmes can overlap. Source ownership, customer deployment and audited financial return are different kinds of evidence.
